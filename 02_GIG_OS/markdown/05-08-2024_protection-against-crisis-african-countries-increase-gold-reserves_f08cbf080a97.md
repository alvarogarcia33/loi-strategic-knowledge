# Protection against crisis: African countries increase gold reserves

Fecha: 05.08.2024
Autor: Redacción de GIG-OS
Categorías: Para la comunidad, Noticias sobre la plataforma, Materiales educativos, Oro, GoalSet Master

Average reading time — 3 minutes

In recent years, many African countries have been actively increasing their gold reserves. The main goal is to protect national economies from various types of financial risks. South Sudan, Tanzania, Uganda and Nigeria are working most intensively to tackle this problem.

Experts believe that the trend for the purchase of the noble metal was set by the central banks of China and India, which regularly purchase investment gold.

Why is gold becoming a preferred asset?

The consequences of the COVID-19 pandemic and growing global instability have weakened African economies, causing disruptions in the supply chains of various goods and raw materials.

In an environment of growing uncertainty, governments opt for gold due to its stability and ability to maintain value over the long term. The precious metal serves as a safe asset that helps protect against economic instability and inflation, which is especially important for developing countries.

Different strategies of African countries

South Sudan

South Sudan is acquiring gold as part of a strategic plan to strengthen the country’s financial stability.

According to the head of the central bank, James Alic Garang, South Sudan is preparing the necessary documents for the purchase and is actively studying the experience of other countries in this field.

Nigeria

Nigerian parliamentarians recommend that the central bank use gold to strengthen and stabilize the national currency, as well as to reduce inflationary threats.

Currently, inflation in the country fluctuates around 35 percent.

Tanzania

The country’s central bank announced plans to allocate $400 million to purchase six tons of gold. Notably, Tanzania produces approximately 50 tons of the noble metal per year, which makes the country the 4th largest gold producer in Africa.

Uganda

The Bank of Uganda intends to purchase gold directly from local gold miners. This decision was made to compensate for the decline in foreign exchange reserves and reduce financial risks.

As of August 5, 2024, the price of an ounce of gold is $2,367.

Conclusions

More and more countries are increasing their gold reserves. This situation highlights the desire of governments to use the noble metal to strengthen the economic system and reduce financial risks.

According to a research by the World Gold Council, around 20 more central banks plan to increase their precious metal reserves next year. This fact once again demonstrates that gold is a strategically important and stable asset, especially during periods of economic instability.
